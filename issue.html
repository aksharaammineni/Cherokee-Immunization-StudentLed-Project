Understanding the Kindergarten Immunization Gap

Although many children in Cherokee County receive recommended vaccinations during early childhood, immunization coverage decreases by the time they reach kindergarten.

According to the Southern Alliance for Public Health Leadership’s Cherokee County profile, only 83.81% of kindergarteners are up to date on their vaccinations.

This is approximately 11.19 percentage points below the 95% public-health benchmark commonly used for community protection.

The early childhood-to-kindergarten gap

Children ages 19–35 months have relatively strong early immunization coverage, but that protection does not consistently carry through to kindergarten entry.

As children grow, they may miss booster doses, families may not update their records, and some parents may have questions or concerns about additional vaccinations.

Why does 95% matter?

The 95% figure is an important public-health benchmark associated with maintaining community protection against highly contagious diseases such as measles.

When vaccination coverage decreases, diseases can spread more easily, especially among infants, children with certain medical conditions, and people who cannot receive certain vaccines.
